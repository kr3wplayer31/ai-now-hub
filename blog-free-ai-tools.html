10 AI Tools That Are Actually Free (or Free to Start)

By The A.I. NOW crew · Detroit · November 2026 · ← Back to THE SIGNAL

⏰ Read this first: free tiers change constantly — companies raise and lower limits all the time. Everything below was the honest state of play when we published in November 2026. Treat the details as "true when written," and glance at each tool's pricing page before you build your whole workflow around a free plan. What does not change: every tool on this list lets you genuinely use it without paying first.

Every week somebody announces an AI tool that will "change everything" — and then asks for $20 a month before you've typed a single word. Here's the truth the ads skip: the biggest AI tools on Earth all have real free tiers, and for most regular people, the free versions are enough to write, draw, edit, research and create for months. Below is what you actually get for $0 from ten of the best — including the catches, because there are always catches.

1. ChatGPT Free tier
What you get: the world's most popular AI chat, free. Ask questions, draft and fix writing, summarize, brainstorm, get help with photos and files, and talk to it by voice. The catch: the free tier runs on usage limits — hit a busy stretch and you'll be asked to wait or offered a paid plan; the newest flagship features sometimes arrive for paying users first. No card needed to start.
Official site →
2. Claude Free tier
What you get: Anthropic's AI, known for careful, natural writing and strong work with long documents — great for contracts, reports and anything where tone matters. The catch: the free plan caps how much you can send in a time window; heavy users hit the wall mid-project. The cap resets, so pacing yourself costs nothing.
Official site →
3. Gemini Free tier
What you get: Google's AI, free with a Google account and built into the Google apps many people already use. Strong at questions that need fresh information, and it can generate and edit images. The catch: image creation and the most advanced models come with daily limits on free, and some features roll out to paid Google AI plans first depending on your country.
Official site →
4. Perplexity Free tier
What you get: an AI search engine that answers your question and shows its sources — the one to reach for when you need to check a fact before acting on it. Standard searches are free and generous. The catch: the deeper "Pro" searches are limited to a small number per day on the free plan, and file uploads and image generation are capped too.
Official site →
5. Canva AI Free plan
What you get: Canva's free plan is a full design studio — flyers, social posts, resumes — with AI helpers (text-to-image, background removal, AI writing) included. The catch: the AI tools draw from a monthly allowance of AI credits on the free plan, and the best stock photos and some premium elements are paid-plan territory. For occasional designs, the allowance goes a long way.
Official site →
6. CapCut Free to start
What you get: a genuinely powerful video editor — phone, computer and web — with AI captions, background removal, and effects, free to download and use. Most everyday editing costs nothing. The catch: a growing shelf of the flashiest AI effects and some export perks sit behind the paid tier, and the app will happily show them to you. What you need for a clean, captioned video is in the free set.
Official site →
7. Leonardo Free tier
What you get: AI image generation with a free plan that refills a daily allowance of image tokens — enough to make real art, product shots and illustrations every day. The catch: free images are generated more slowly, are public by default, and come with a license meant for personal use — commercial use is what the paid plans are for. Read that part before you print anything for sale.
Official site →
8. Ideogram Free plan
What you get: the image generator that's famously good at one thing others fumble: words inside pictures. Posters, logos, shirts, signs with text spelled right. The free plan hands you a small batch of credits that refresh on a cycle. The catch: that batch runs out fast and free generations wait in the slow queue — plan your best ideas before you click.
Official site →
9. ElevenLabs Small free tier
What you get: the most realistic AI voices anywhere — type words, get natural speech, in many languages. The free plan includes a small monthly allowance of characters so you can hear what it can do. The catch: that allowance is genuinely small — think testing and short clips, not audiobooks — and the free plan is for non-commercial projects. It's a demo with real value, not a workhorse. House rule: only ever clone a voice you own or have clear permission to use.
Official site →
10. Suno Free tier
What you get: type a description — "a soul song about missing Detroit" — and Suno writes and performs a full song, vocals and all. The free plan refills credits daily, enough for a handful of songs a day. The catch: songs you make on the free plan are licensed for non-commercial use only — have fun, share with friends, but if a track is ever going on a release or an ad, that's paid-plan (and read the terms) territory.
Official site →

How to actually use this list

Don't sign up for all ten today. Pick by the job: writing and questions → ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Checking facts → Perplexity. Pictures → Leonardo, Ideogram, or Canva. Video → CapCut. Voices → ElevenLabs. Music → Suno. Live inside the free tiers for a month and you'll know — from your own use, not an ad — whether any tool has earned your money. Most people find the free versions cover them. That's not a loophole; it's the business model: these companies would rather have you hooked for free than not at all.

One last honesty note: "free" tools are paid for somehow — usually by the hope you'll upgrade, and sometimes by the data in your account. Don't paste anything into any AI that you wouldn't want stored on a company's server: no passwords, no bank details, no private documents about other people. Free is a great deal. It's just not a private one.
