🧳 AI for travel, explained

Five ways AI already shows up in a trip — what each one is genuinely good at, and the catch nobody puts in the ads. Balanced on purpose.

🗺️ AI itinerary builders
Give an AI your dates, budget and pace and it drafts a day-by-day plan in seconds — neighborhoods, sights, even rough timing. The catch: it plans from patterns in old information, so it can cheerfully schedule a museum on its closed day or a restaurant that shut down last year. Use it as a strong first draft, then check every hour, price and address on the official site before you build your day around it.
💸 Price-prediction & fare-watching AI
Tools like Hopper and Google Flights study fare history and nudge you: "prices usually rise soon" or "this is likely to drop." Fare watching is genuinely useful — set the alert and let it stare at the price so you don't have to. The honest limit: a prediction is an educated guess, not a promise. Fares jump for reasons no model sees coming, so treat "buy now" advice as a weather forecast, not a guarantee.
🗣️ Translation AI
Point your camera at a sign or menu and read it in your language; hold a two-way conversation with a stranger through the phone. For menus, signs, directions and polite small talk, this is close to magic. The limit: nuance, slang, dialect and anything legal or medical can come out wrong — download the offline language pack before you go, and for anything that truly matters, get a human to confirm.
🕵️ Fake reviews & booking scams
Review sections are full of bought praise, and scam sites copy real hotels almost perfectly. AI can help you read smarter: scan for reviews that repeat the same phrases, check whether praise sounds like an ad, and cross-check a "deal" against the hotel's own site. Your best defenses cost nothing: book through the official site or app, be suspicious of prices that beat everyone by a mile, and never pay by gift card or wire transfer.
📱 AI assistants in airline & hotel apps
When a flight gets canceled, the airline app's assistant can rebook you, reissue a boarding pass and answer baggage questions at 2 a.m. — often faster than the phone line. Hotels use the same tech for check-in and requests. The limit: bots handle the routine stuff and fumble the weird stuff. If your situation involves money, a missed connection or a mistake the airline made, push politely for a human — you're allowed.

📋 The Trip Planner Prompt Pack — plan it with AI, check it like a skeptic

Hit Copy, paste into ChatGPT, Gemini or any AI chat, and swap the [brackets] for your details. Then verify anything important at the official source — the pack is a planner, not a travel agent.

Draft my itinerary

Draft a [number]-day itinerary for [destination] for [number] travelers, at a relaxed pace, with a total budget of about $[amount] not counting flights. Keep each day in one neighborhood or area so we're not crossing town all day. Mix one big sight with downtime, note anything that usually needs booking in advance, and mark anything you're unsure about so I can verify it before I count on it.

Split my trip budget

I have $[amount] total for a [number]-day trip to [destination] for [number] people, after flights. Split it into a simple daily budget: lodging, food, local transport, activities, and a cushion for surprises. Show it as a table, flag the category most likely to blow up, and give me two honest ways to trim if money gets tight. No made-up prices — use ranges and tell me to check real prices live.

Build my packing list

Build me a packing list for [number] days in [destination] in [month], [carry-on only / checked bag OK]. Typical weather that time of year, walking-heavy days, one nicer dinner. Group it by: wear on the plane, in the bag, documents & money, chargers & tech, and a short "glad I brought it" section of small things people forget. Keep it realistic — if it doesn't fit a carry-on, say so.

Local phrases that matter

Teach me the 20 most useful phrases for a trip to [destination], in [language]: hello, please, thank you, excuse me, where is…?, how much?, the check please, water, bathroom, help, emergency, I don't understand, slower please, taxi phrases, and ordering food. For each: the phrase, how to say it out loud in simple English sounds, and one short note on local politeness if it matters.

Here is a trip plan another AI made for me. Read it like a skeptic: flag anything that might not exist, sounds closed or out of season, assumes impossible timing, or needs a booking I haven't made.
