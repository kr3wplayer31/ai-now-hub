🎨 Create anything, explained

Five ways people are creating with AI right now — plus the honest rules that come with it. No hype: what these tools do well, and where they stop.

🎨 AI image art
Type a description — "a neon-lit Detroit street at night, painted like a movie poster" — and get a picture back in seconds. Don't like it? Change a word and run it again. It's how people make posters, book covers, product mockups and art prints today. The skill is in the describing: subject, style, lighting, mood.
🎬 AI video
Describe a shot, or hand over a still image, and AI turns it into a short moving clip. Today's clips run seconds, not scenes — so creators plan in shots and stitch them together in an editor. It's already changing music videos, ads and short-form content, and the tools double as smart editing assistants.
🎵 AI music
Name a genre, a mood and a topic — "soulful R&B about missing your hometown" — and get a full song back: melody, instruments and, if you want, a sung vocal. Great for demos, background tracks and hearing an idea before you book a studio. Your taste in picking and editing is still the difference.
🎙️ AI voices
Type it, hear it spoken — narration for videos, audiobooks and lessons in voices that sound human. One firm rule: only clone a voice that's yours, or one you have clear permission to use. Cloning someone else's voice without consent isn't a gray area — don't do it.
✍️ AI writing help
A brainstorming partner that never sleeps: outlines, first drafts, titles, rewrites and editing passes. You stay the author — it hands you clay, you do the sculpting. Always read what it gives you, check facts it states, and rewrite in your own voice. Readers can smell unedited AI text, and so can editors.
⚖️ The honest rules
Copyright for AI-made work is still being sorted out. In the US, purely AI-generated material generally can't be copyrighted on its own — human creative input is what the law protects, and the rules differ by country and keep changing. Platforms also have their own rules, and many require disclosure. Don't copy a living artist's style and sell it as theirs, don't pass AI work off as hand-made, and when in doubt, ask a lawyer before you sell.
🤝 The A.I. NOW house rule: AI-made work should be labeled as AI-made. Say so in the caption, the credits or the description — your audience deserves to know, platforms increasingly require it, and honesty is the whole brand.

📋 The Creator Prompt Pack — copy-ready prompts for images, video, songs & hooks

Hit Copy, paste into the tool named on each prompt, and swap the [brackets] for your own idea. Then make it yours — edit, pick, polish. That's the creator part.

🖼️ Album or book cover art Image

Design cover art for a [genre] project called "[title]". Subject: [what's in the picture]. Mood: [mood — e.g. hopeful, gritty, dreamy]. Style: bold colors, strong lighting, cinematic. Leave clean space at the top for the title text — do not put words in the image.

🖼️ A character with a story Image

A character portrait of [describe your character — age, look, outfit, one detail that hints at their story], in the style of [watercolor painting / 3D animation / vintage comic book], standing in [place], [time of day] light, expressive face, detailed background.

🎬 A five-second scene Video

A 5-second cinematic shot: [subject — e.g. a lone skater] in [location] at [time of day]. The camera slowly pushes in. [Weather or atmosphere detail]. Mood: [mood]. Photorealistic, smooth motion, no text or logos.

🎬 Turn a photo into motion Video

Animate the attached image: [what should move — e.g. the clouds drift, the water ripples, her hair blows in the wind]. Keep everything else still and unchanged. Gentle, natural movement, loop-friendly.

🎵 A song from one sentence Music

A [genre] song about [topic]. The hook repeats the line "[your hook line]". Verses tell this story: [one or two sentences of the story]. [Tempo and feel — e.g. upbeat, warm, made for a car ride with the windows down].

🎵 Lyrics first, music second Music

Write song lyrics about [topic] in a [genre] style. Two verses and a chorus. Simple, honest words a real person would say — no clichés about "neon hearts." Give the chorus one line people will remember after one listen.

🪝 Hooks that stop the scroll Writing

Give me 10 opening lines for a short video about [topic] for [who it's for]. Each under 12 words, each honest — no clickbait lies, no fake promises. Mix it up: 4 questions, 3 bold statements, 3 "I wish I'd known…" starters.

🪝 Captions in your own voice Writing

Write 3 captions for a post showing [what you made]. Casual and human, like I'd text a friend. One funny, one proud, one that asks my followers a real question. If any part of it was made with AI, add a short honest line saying so.
