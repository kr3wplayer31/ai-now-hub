🌱 AI in daily life, explained

Six places AI already shows up in an ordinary day — what each one is genuinely good at, and where its limits are. Balanced on purpose.

🩺 AI health apps
What they can do: explain symptoms and medical words in plain language, help you track habits like sleep, steps and medication reminders, and help you write down good questions before an appointment.
What they can't do: examine you, run tests, or diagnose you. A symptom checker is a starting point for a conversation — never the final word. Anything worrying goes to a real clinician.
🏠 Smart home AI
Voice assistants, smart thermostats, doorbell cameras and robot vacuums use AI to learn routines — warming the house before you get home, flagging a package at the door, turning lights on with your voice. The trade-off: these devices listen and collect data to work, so check the privacy settings on anything with a microphone or camera, and keep the apps updated.
🎓 AI tutors & learning
AI can explain a hard idea five different ways until one clicks, quiz you before a test, and practice a language with you at 2 a.m. without getting tired. The limit: it can state wrong things with total confidence — so treat it like a study partner, not the textbook. If it matters for a grade, a license or a job, verify it against a trusted source.
⚖️ AI legal-doc explainers
Paste in a confusing lease, letter or form and AI can translate the legal words into plain English, point at deadlines, and list the questions worth asking. The hard limit: it is information only — not a lawyer and not legal advice. It can miss things and it doesn't know your situation. For court dates, evictions, contracts or anything with real consequences, a real lawyer or legal-aid office is the move.
🛒 AI shopping assistants
Shopping AIs compare products, summarize piles of reviews, watch for price drops and answer "which one fits my budget?" in seconds. Watch out: some assistants push sponsored products or whoever pays for placement, and summaries can repeat review mistakes. Use them to narrow the field — then check the price and the return policy yourself before you buy.
✈️ AI travel planners
Tell an AI your dates, budget and pace and it will draft a day-by-day itinerary, suggest neighborhoods to stay in, and flag long layovers. Before you rely on it: double-check opening hours, prices and booking details at the official sites — AI can invent a restaurant that closed or a train that doesn't run. Great first draft, not a travel agent.

📋 The AI for Life Prompt Pack — prompts for real life

Hit Copy, paste into ChatGPT, Gemini or any AI chat, and swap the [brackets] for your details.

Understand a confusing letter or document

Explain the document below in plain English, like you're talking to a smart neighbor. Define every hard word, list any deadlines or dollar amounts, tell me exactly what actions it asks for, and finish with the questions I should ask a professional before I respond. Information only — don't give me legal or medical advice. [PASTE THE LETTER OR DOCUMENT HERE]

Plan meals on a budget

Make me a 5-day dinner plan for [number] people with a grocery budget of $[amount]. Simple ingredients from any grocery store, about 30 minutes a meal or less, and give me one shopping list sorted by aisle. If I tell you foods I can't eat, keep them out completely.

Build my study plan

Build me a study plan to learn [subject] in [number] days, with [minutes] minutes a day. One clear goal per day, a short quiz at the end of each day to check I actually got it, and a review day every fourth day. Start from zero — I know nothing yet.

Compare two products honestly

Compare [product A] and [product B] for someone who mainly cares about [what matters to you — price, durability, ease of use]. Give me a simple table of the real differences, who each one is better for, and what I should check in person or in reviews before buying. No hype, no sponsored-sounding language — if they're basically the same, say so.

Get ready for a doctor's appointment

Help me prepare for a doctor's appointment. I'll describe what's been going on; turn it into a short, clear timeline of symptoms and a list of questions to ask the doctor, in the order a busy doctor would want to hear them. Preparation only — do not try to diagnose me or tell me what's wrong. [DESCRIBE WHAT'S BEEN GOING ON HERE]

Draft a trip plan

Draft a [number]-day trip plan for [destination] on a budget of about $[amount], at a relaxed pace. One neighborhood or area per day so I'm not crossing town all day, a mix of one big sight and downtime, and note anything I must book in advance. Mark anything you're unsure about so I can verify it before I count on it.
